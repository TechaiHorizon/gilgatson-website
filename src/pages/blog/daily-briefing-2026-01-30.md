• **[Wisconsin communities signed secrecy deals for billion-dollar data centers](https://www.wpr.org/news/4-wisconsin-communities-signed-secrecy-deals-billion-dollar-data-centers)** - The cloak-and-dagger approach to data center deals exposes a deeper anxiety about local backlash and geopolitical risk. These secrecy agreements underscore how critical and sensitive semiconductor-adjacent infrastructure has become amid US-China tensions, with communities effectively sidelined while corporate and government interests prioritize strategic control over transparency. This sets a worrying precedent for public oversight in the tech supply chain’s physical backbone[1].

• **[Tesla's Robotaxi data confirms crash rate 3x worse than humans even with monitor](https://electrek.co/2026/01/29/teslas-own-robotaxi-data-confirms-crash-rate-3x-worse-than-humans-even-with-monitor/)** - The lofty promises of Tesla’s autonomous ambitions collide with harsh reality here. The tripled crash rate despite human oversight reveals that AI-driven mobility remains a risky bet, undermining US claims of technological superiority in AI safety and reliability. This data will embolden China’s push to close the self-driving gap, as the US’s leading-edge hype fails to translate into robust real-world performance.

• **[Netflix Animation Studios Joins the Blender Development Fund as Corporate Patron](https://www.blender.org/press/netflix-animation-studios-joins-the-blender-development-fund-as-corporate-patron/)** - While seemingly a creative industry move, Netflix’s investment in open-source 3D graphics tools like Blender is a strategic play to wrest control from proprietary software giants dominated by US and European firms. This signals a subtle but important shift toward democratizing AI-driven content creation tools, potentially disrupting entrenched corporate ecosystems and leveling the field for innovation outside traditional Silicon Valley oligopolies.

• **[OpenClaw – Moltbot Renamed Again](https://openclaw.ai/blog/introducing-openclaw)** - The continual rebranding and repositioning of this AI platform reflects the hypercompetitive, volatile nature of AI startups chasing relevance amid a crowded landscape. More critically, OpenClaw’s focus on open-source AI tooling highlights a geopolitical tug-of-war between centralized US cloud monopolies and decentralized innovation models that may empower China and other players to bypass Western gatekeepers.

• **[Norway EV Push Nears 100 Percent: What's Next?](https://spectrum.ieee.org/norway-ev-policy-electric-vehicles)** - Norway’s near-total EV adoption is less about green idealism and more about strategic positioning in global semiconductor supply chains, especially for EV chips. As EVs become the battlefield for semiconductor demand, Norway’s market signals where raw materials and chip-dependent industries will concentrate next—creating choke points that China will aggressively target to undermine Western automotive and energy security.

• **[Software Pump and Dump](http://tautvilas.lt/software-pump-and-dump/)** - The rampant hype cycles inflating AI startups resemble financial pump-and-dump schemes, risking a collapse that will ripple through tech markets and policy frameworks alike. This financialization of AI distracts from genuine technological progress and exposes investors and governments to systemic risk just as China accelerates state-backed, long-term AI development strategies immune to short-term market theatrics.

• **[Code is cheap. Show me the talk](https://nadh.in/blog/code-is-cheap/)** - The myth that code alone drives innovation is exposed as naive; strategic narrative and deployment matter more for geopolitical advantage. This underscores why the US-China tech war is not just about talent and patents, but about who controls the story, ecosystem, and standards that shape global AI adoption—and why rushing code without strategic context is a losing game.
