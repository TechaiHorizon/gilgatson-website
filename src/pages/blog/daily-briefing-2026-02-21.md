**Sources: OpenAI is telling investors it's targeting ~$600B in total compute spend by 2030, months after Sam Altman touted $1.4T in infrastructure commitments**  
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/20/openai-resets-spend-expectations-targets-around-600-billion-by-2030.html  
OpenAI’s abrupt halving of its total compute spend target signals more than just financial prudence—it exposes the overhyped AI arms race narrative. Investors were sold a $1.4 trillion infrastructure investment that now looks inflated, raising questions about the sustainability of AI’s exponential growth claims. This reset hints at technological or economic ceilings in AI compute scalability that mainstream hype conveniently ignores.

**Phil Spencer will retire after 38 years at Microsoft; Asha Sharma, the president of product in Microsoft's Core AI business, will become the CEO of gaming**  
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/20/microsoft-gaming-chief-phil-spencer-retires-asha-sharma-replacing.html  
Spencer’s departure after nearly four decades could mark a seismic shift in Microsoft’s gaming strategy, especially with an AI product veteran taking the helm. It’s a clear pivot toward AI-driven gaming integration, but it also risks alienating traditional gamers who fear the dilution of genuine gameplay in favor of AI-generated content and monetization schemes. Microsoft is betting that AI can save gaming’s stagnating innovation, but that gamble could backfire if it underestimates gamer backlash.

**Microsoft Gaming CEO Asha Sharma says she is committed to “the return of Xbox” and that the company won't “flood our ecosystem with soulless AI slop”**  
https://www.theverge.com/games/882326/read-microsoft-gaming-ceo-asha-sharma-first-memo  
Sharma’s vow to avoid “soulless AI slop” is a rare admission that the industry has been flooding consumers with low-quality AI-generated content. Yet, her promise clashes with Microsoft’s broader AI ambitions, suggesting internal tension between authentic creative values and profit-driven AI scalability. The real question: can Microsoft maintain this delicate balance, or is this just PR to placate skeptical gamers while quietly ramping up AI-driven monetization?

**Modi pitches India as an artificial intelligence hub at the AI summit**  
https://apnews.com/article/india-ai-summit-modi-artificial-intelligence-67c2b5a37f98e0a6ebb81136e0287969  
India’s push to become an AI hub is less about innovation leadership and more about leveraging cheap data and labor to serve Western tech giants. Modi’s pitch glosses over the glaring tech infrastructure gaps and the country’s dependence on foreign AI models. The geopolitical risk is India becoming a data supplier rather than a tech innovator, entrenching digital colonialism under the guise of AI progress.

**India eyes $200B in data center investments as it ramps up its AI hub ambitions**  
https://apnews.com/article/india-modi-vaishnaw-ai-investments-4e170da0a3b883a9659569dd538e9019  
Massive data center investments are touted as a pillar for India’s AI ambitions, but this heavy capital influx risks creating an infrastructure bubble primed to serve foreign AI monopolies instead of domestic innovation. The lack of a clear strategy to build indigenous AI capabilities means India might become a mere extension of US and Chinese cloud dominance, locking in dependency rather than independence.
