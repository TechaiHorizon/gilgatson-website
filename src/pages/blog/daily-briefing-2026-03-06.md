**Sources: the US is considering requiring countries whose companies buy large volumes of Nvidia and AMD AI chips to invest in US AI infrastructure**  
https://www.ft.com/content/30d0b1df-ad8c-42eb-852b-dad53acaecfc  
Washington’s move to tie AI chip purchases to mandatory investments in US AI infrastructure isn’t just economic leverage—it’s a blunt instrument of geopolitical control masquerading as trade policy. Forcing foreign buyers into infrastructure commitments signals an aggressive attempt to entrench US dominance in AI supply chains, but risks alienating vital allies and driving chip consumers towards clandestine or alternative suppliers, potentially accelerating the very tech decoupling the US claims to prevent. The policy’s heavy-handedness could fracture global AI cooperation under the guise of safeguarding national security.  

**Sources: US officials propose expanding AI chip export controls globally, requiring Commerce Department approval for Nvidia and AMD shipments for each country**  
https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-05/us-drafts-rules-for-sweeping-power-over-nvidia-s-global-sales  
This unprecedented push to micromanage every Nvidia and AMD chip shipment underlines the US’s deepening paranoia about China’s AI ambitions, but it also exposes the fragility of the global semiconductor ecosystem. These sweeping export controls are likely to disrupt legitimate commercial flows, stifle innovation, and provoke retaliatory measures from other nations seeking to break US tech dominance. The corporate victims here are caught in the crossfire of Washington’s strategic overreach, with the unintended consequence of accelerating the diversification of chip manufacturing hubs beyond US influence.

The gargantuan $110 billion bankroll funneled into OpenAI by tech giants reads less like a confident investment and more like a desperate scramble to control the AI narrative and infrastructure. This money tsunami inflates OpenAI into a near-monopolistic powerhouse, threatening to crush smaller competitors and entrench a narrow elite controlling AI’s future. The concentration of power here amplifies risks of groupthink, stifles diverse innovation, and hands over critical societal decision-making influence to a few hyper-capitalized players swimming in conflicts of interest.
