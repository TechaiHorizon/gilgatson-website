**Super Micro says co-founder Yih-Shyan Liaw has resigned from its board after US prosecutors indicted him on allegations of smuggling Nvidia AI chips to China**  
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/20/super-micro-co-founder-leaves-board.html  
The swift resignation of Yih-Shyan Liaw exposes the uncomfortable reality that US-China tech tensions have now penetrated the highest ranks of American hardware firms. This isn’t an isolated compliance slip but rather a symptom of the porous enforcement environment around dual-use AI semiconductor technologies. The board shakeup signals increased regulatory scrutiny ahead but also highlights the vulnerability of US supply chains to insider risks amid geopolitical crackdowns.

**US prosecutors charge three people affiliated with Super Micro, including co-founder Yih-Shyan Liaw, with smuggling Nvidia AI chips to China**  
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/19/us-tech-execs-smuggled-nvidia-chips-to-china-prosecutors-say.html  
The indictments underscore how lucrative and covert the black market for advanced AI chips has become, despite Washington’s aggressive export controls. Prosecuting individuals is a reactive band-aid that fails to address the systemic incentives for tech transfer to China, where domestic AI ambitions are fueled by illicit access to Western hardware. This indicates that enforcement alone cannot stop China’s AI ascendance; the US must rethink how it defines “national security” in the semiconductor era.

This oligopoly heightens systemic risk as innovation funnels through a narrow set of corporate gatekeepers, potentially stifling diversity of approaches and amplifying geopolitical leverage by dominant US tech giants.
